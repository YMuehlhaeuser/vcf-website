Making international AI agreements verifiable.

Containing the most serious risks from advanced AI will likely require agreement between governments. Any such agreement is only as good as each party's ability to verify that the others are abiding by it. The Verifiable Compute Foundation is an independent nonprofit working to make that verification possible.

Work with us What we do

Arms control worked because each side could check the other. The INF Treaty came with on-site inspections, data exchanges and continuous monitoring outside missile plants.

Advanced AI has nothing like it yet. Verification systems take years to build, so the work has to start before an agreement does.

Why now

Governments are discussing limits on AI. Verification isn't ready.

Agreements are on the table

Officials in Washington, Beijing and other capitals are discussing limits on the most dangerous AI development. Whatever they agree will need to be checked.

The tools aren't ready

There are promising methods for tracking chips, monitoring datacenters and proving what a computation did. Most are untested at scale, and none is ready to underwrite a treaty.

No one owns the whole problem

Researchers, startups, AI developers, chipmakers, funders and governments each hold a piece. VCF exists to make sure the pieces fit together.

What we do

We make sure verification is ready in time.

We don't build verification systems ourselves. We set the agenda, fund and commission the work, provide the infrastructure it needs and bring the right people together. We have no product to sell and no single technology to push.

  1. 01

    Set the agenda

    We publish the field's shared plan: the open research problems and who is working on them, costed project proposals for funders and founders, and the definitions any agreement will need.

  2. 02

    Fund and commission

    We commission targeted research and direct funding to the projects that close the most important gaps, from satellite monitoring to semiconductor supply chains.

  3. 03

    Provide shared infrastructure

    We run a free research cluster with the hardware access commercial clouds don't grant, and arrange independent red-teaming of verification designs.

  4. 04

    Advise and convene

    We help governments understand what can be verified, how confidently and at what cost, and bring the field together in working sessions that produce something.

Research cluster

Datacenter hardware, open to verification researchers.

Verification research needs access that commercial clouds don't give: bare metal, firmware and the machine itself. Our cluster provides it free of charge to anyone working on AI verification, and our training programs bring new cohorts onto it.
